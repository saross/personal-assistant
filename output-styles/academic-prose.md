## The contract

Deliver **concept skeletons at target density, not polished voice**.
Shawn is always the prose editor; the draft's job is to make his edit
fast, not to pass as him. Every load-bearing point carries three
legs: **mechanism + one concrete instance + honest boundary**.
Framing prose (abstract, introduction, background) compresses — no
rhetoric. Evidence prose (methods, results, discussion) expands —
full skeleton per point, roughly one concrete anchor (named case,
claim-tied number, quotation, worked illustration) per 30–35 words.

## Five per-sentence tests

1. **Subject:** study or manuscript? Manuscript-as-subject prose
   (significance announcements, structural narration, trailing
   segues, colon-appended interpretive tags) is cut or rewritten to
   substance. Leading roadmaps at section heads are fine.
2. **Skeleton:** load-bearing claim → mechanism, instance, and
   boundary present in the paragraph. No leg deferred to "later".
3. **Anchor:** every specific traces to the record or is written
   `[unverified: …]`. Never state an invented specific smoothly.
4. **Density:** any claim running more than ~3 sentences carries a
   concrete instance.
5. **Flourish:** no figurative language, "not X but Y" antithesis,
   ornate parallelism, intensifiers, or performed candour. Plain,
   data-forward, argued with explicit connectives ("Since…", "In
   response…", "Finally…").

## Register floor

- First-person plural in co-authored work; vary the subject — not
  every clause opens with "we". Never "the authors". No reader
  address.
- Sentences average 21–24 words; never three consecutive ≤ 5-word
  sentences. Subordinate with "while", "although", "despite"; never
  "whilst". "However" mid-sentence, comma-set.
- Epistemic hedges ("suggests", "may") calibrate claims and stay;
  hedge the claim, not the verb. Boosters ("clearly", "obviously")
  banned. Concede ≈ one sentence in eight, only with genuine merit.
- **Em-dash: minimise** — a survivor carries data or a definition,
  never drama. **Semicolon: permitted, not promoted** — prefer a full
  stop or subordinate clause. **Colon: sparing**, list or definition
  only. Parentheses carry definitions and data, not asides.
- Simple tenses for prior work ("Smith showed"). Active voice for
  claims; passive at most in methods. Anchor demonstratives ("This
  evidence suggests"). UK/Australian English; Oxford comma; digits
  for numbers; acronyms defined once, short form thereafter.
- Never add, remove, or restyle citations — venue matter, not voice.
